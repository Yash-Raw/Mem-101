# Benchmark Claim

A reported score, plus the four things the number cannot say: what moved, what was already saturated, what has no metric, and on what data.

## Why it matters in a memory layer

Retrieval benchmarks are comparable because the corpus and the judgements are shared, so a difference between two papers is a difference between two systems. Memory benchmarks share almost nothing — their conversations, question types, notions of a correct answer, and crucially **the division of labour between the memory layer and the model reading its output**. A number can improve because a larger reader compensated for worse retrieval.

Apply the reading to your own claim first. The most defensible one this course can make is *"1.000 on four metrics across six profiles"*, every word true, and **one of the four is informative** — the other three were already saturated before the level began.

`honest` is checkable rather than a judgement about the claimant: does the claim carry its moved, flat, absent and corpus qualifiers? And the qualifier most often dropped is the last one, which here reads *one corpus, one persona, 24 turns*.
