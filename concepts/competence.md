# Competence

What a given writer is authoritative *about* — trust attached to the claim rather than to the claimant.

## Why it matters in a memory layer

A retrieval source is reputable across its output; a memory writer is not. A calendar agent is excellent about calendars and, about a user's diet, no better informed than a stranger. `Provenance.authority` is a constant on the row, so it cannot express that.

Competence is a short table of writer → slots, and it needs **three** outcomes rather than two. Within the domain, keep the authority. Naming a slot the writer does not own, discount it. Naming **no** modelled slot at all, keep the authority and *flag it* — because that claim is outside the vocabulary, not outside the writer's domain, and discounting it would record a gap in your slot table as a fact about the writer's reliability.

On this course's corpus that distinction is the whole result: the calendar agent's entire output is unnameable, so a two-state policy drops the reliable writer to 0.3 and leaves the unreliable one exactly where it was.
